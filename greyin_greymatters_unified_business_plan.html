1. Four-Pillar Architecture

1. GREYIN (Enterprise B2B Portal)

The monetization frontend for enterprise clients, OEMs, and hiring partners. Offers age-blind candidate search, fractional leadership placement, and corporate outplacement tools.

2. GREYMATTERS (Thought Leadership Blog)

The content engine anchored around the mission slogan "Because Grey Matters." Publishes deep domain teardowns, system design blueprints, and career transition guides to drive organic SEO and candidate signups.

3. SALT & PEPPER (Community Lounge)

Private, age-verified peer community for senior pros (12+ Yrs). Features unfiltered architecture reviews, "The Lab" project showcase, and peer referral networks free from recruiters.

4. FREEAGENT (0%-Commission Freelancing)

Zero-fee gig engine empowering community members to consult, contract, or review peer projects with 100% earnings retention and zero platform middleman fees.

3. Content Strategy: The "GreyMatters" Editorial Engine

The GreyMatters blog functions as the primary organic top-of-funnel (TOFU) acquisition engine for both senior talent and hiring executives:

  • Architecture Teardowns: High-level system design breakdowns (e.g., "AUTOSAR Zonal Architecture vs. Centralized Compute," "Local LLM Deployments on On-Prem Hardware").
  • Career & Seniority Insights: Thought pieces on ageism in tech, transitioning from embedded hardware to AI/ML, and navigating fractional leadership roles after 15+ years.
  • Community Spotlights: Highlighting projects and MVPs built by members inside Salt & Pepper Lounge, driving traffic directly to the FreeAgent freelance engine.

4. Technical Infrastructure (As Built: Self-Hosted Supabase + Next.js)

The original plan called for a self-hosted NocoBase 2.0 instance. In implementation this was superseded by self-hosted Supabase (PostgreSQL, GoTrue auth, PostgREST, Kong API gateway, and S3-compatible Storage) behind four independent Next.js 14 App Router frontends — one per pillar — sharing a single Postgres database. The change was made for better authentication primitives (email-OTP verified signup, password reset), row-level-security-enforced data access in place of application-level permission checks, and object storage for user-uploaded images, none of which NocoBase 2.0 offered out of the box. Data sovereignty is preserved: everything still runs self-hosted on the same infrastructure, behind Docker Swarm and Traefik with automatic Let's Encrypt TLS.

What's live today:

  1. Editorial engine: the posts table plus an in-app author dashboard (write, edit, publish, cover-image upload) — not a Markdown-collection admin UI, a purpose-built CMS behind each author's own login, gated by role the same way every other pillar-specific action is.
  2. Public delivery: published posts, categories, and search are server-rendered Next.js pages, not an embedded NocoBase view — same SEO-friendly outcome, different rendering path.
  3. Lead-capture routing: newsletter signup and category/search-driven discovery are live on GreyMatters; cross-pillar routing (a GreyMatters reader landing in Salt & Pepper or a Greyin consultation) is manual/link-based today rather than a shared-identity handoff — see the Section 4 note below.
  4. Payments: Razorpay integration for FreeAgent checkout, currently running in test mode pending a deliberate switch to live keys.
  5. Notifications: a cross-pillar in-app notification system, fed by Postgres triggers on the events users actually care about (new application, application status change, order status change, new order message, new comment, new discussion reply, payout status change) — this wasn't in the original plan at all, added because the underlying event data already existed in every pillar's tables and surfacing it in-app closes a real engagement gap.

Known architectural gap: each of the four Next.js apps currently has its own independent user base (a Salt & Pepper member and a FreeAgent freelancer with the same person behind them are two unrelated accounts today), even though they share one Supabase project. Building true cross-pillar identity — a single login carrying a member from GreyMatters readership into Salt & Pepper membership into a Greyin placement, which is the flywheel this plan describes — is the highest-leverage engineering gap against the strategy as written. See COMPETITIVE_BENCHMARK.md, "Cross-cutting gaps," item 1.

5. Flywheel Dynamics & Growth Engine

  1. Read (GreyMatters): Practitioners and hiring VPs read authoritative, deep-domain technical articles on the GreyMatters blog.
  2. Engage (Salt & Pepper): Senior talent joins the gated lounge to discuss articles, showcase MVPs, and collaborate.
  3. Build & Earn (FreeAgent): Members offer micro-consulting and accept peer gigs at 0% platform commission.
  4. Hire (Greyin): Enterprise buyers hire verified senior talent for fractional or advisory roles, generating platform revenue.
